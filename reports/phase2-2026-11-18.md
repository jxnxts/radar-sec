Pass criterion (fixed before the test run): median per-signal excess > 0 against SPY and IWM, and Newey-West t ≥ 2 of the mean monthly excess of the calendar portfolio against SPY and IWM, at the 3-month horizon, in 2023–2025. "maybe" = positive mean monthly excess against both benchmarks in train and in test, with test t between 1 and 2 or a pass on only one benchmark.

Closest call: R2 `big_officer_buy` (the phase-1 rule, unchanged) earned +0.50% a month over IWM in 2023–2025 (t +2.3), but −0.18% against SPY (t −0.3) and a negative median excess against both. It beat small caps, not the market, and the typical signal still lost: it fails the pre-registered rule. It was not a candidate in train either (t −0.5 against IWM).

The 10-K text signal went the wrong way in the test period: the least-changed quintile (V8) lagged SPY by a significant margin, and the top-minus-bottom spread that was positive in train turned negative. An equal-weighted basket of US stocks above US$ 300 million lagged a cap-weighted SPY led by a handful of mega-caps in 2023–2025; the text signal did not overcome that.

## Survivorship correction

Phase-1 signals 2020–2025: 9,018 generated. At 3 months, Yahoo alone measures 6,656 (74%); with the SEC-based correction, 7,454 (83%). 1,564 remain unmeasured, mostly companies that left the exchange years after the signal and have no insider trade near the horizon date.

What happened to the companies without Yahoo prices, from their own SEC filings: acquisition 1,160, active 611, voluntary delisting 365, bankruptcy 204, stopped filing 17, unknown 1.

How the corrected 3-month exits were valued: tape 772, tape last 21, merger doc cash 4, bankruptcy 1. Most terminal events (mergers, bankruptcies) happen long after a 3- or 6-month horizon, so the price tape does most of the work.

The 798 signals measured only thanks to the correction did **worse** than the rest: median 3-month excess vs SPY −6.4%, 36% beat the index. Putting them back moves the median excess of all phase-1 signals from −3.2% to −3.5%. Survivorship bias was flattering phase 1, not hiding a winner.

Price tape accuracy (checked on signals Yahoo does price, see plan addendum b): median error about +1% against Yahoo, 90th percentile absolute error about 20%. The tape is a noisy but unbiased-in-the-median substitute; treat corrected numbers as approximate.

- test: top-minus-bottom quintile spread −0.32% a month (t −0.8, 36 months).

## Limitations

1. **Survivorship correction is approximate.** It uses Form 4 prices (noisy, about 20% error at the 90th percentile), merger considerations from Form 4 code D or proxy text, and −100% for bankruptcies. About 17% of phase-1 signals remain unmeasured.
2. **The text universe is survivorship-biased**: it starts from today's SEC ticker list and Yahoo, so companies that disappeared before 2026 are missing. Only 10-Ks were compared (no 10-Q), on the whole document; the hidden inline-XBRL header stayed in the text (mostly numbers, which are dropped).
3. **Drawdown needs Yahoo history**, so issuers without it can never be a V4/V6 signal.
4. **Execution is idealized**: close-to-close, fixed costs (0.2%, 1% below US$ 5), no market impact, no taxes. Microcaps would cost more.
5. **Three years of test data** is 36 monthly observations: an effect of 0.3% a month would need years more to reach t = 2. The test rejects strong effects; it cannot prove a small one is absent.
6. **Opportunistic classification** follows Cohen, Malloy & Pomorski and only covers insiders with three prior years of trades (about 13% of base filings).
7. **Code P is not only open-market buying**: private placements and offerings at a fixed price also use it.
