## How to read

- **Return**: from the close of the first session after the filing to N months later, net of a 0.2% cost (round trip).
- **Excess**: the signal's return minus the return of SPY over the same dates. It is the column that answers "was it worth more than buying the index?".
- **Hit rate**: share of signals with a positive net return. **Beats SPY**: share with a positive excess.
- **Median**: the result of the typical signal. When the mean is good and the median is bad, the gain came from a few stocks that exploded.
- **Mean excess (winsor.)**: the mean after capping the extremes at the 1st and 99th percentiles.
- **t (monthly cohort)**: t statistic of the monthly mean excess. Above 2 suggests the effect is not chance; below that, nothing can be claimed.

## Rules and assumptions

- `cluster_buy`: at least 2 distinct insiders with an open-market buy (code P) in the same issuer within 14 days, adding up to at least $100K.
- `big_officer_buy`: a CEO or CFO buys at least $250K on the open market in a single Form 4.
- After a signal, the same type does not fire again for the same issuer for 30 days.
- The signal is dated by the **SEC filing**, never by the transaction date. Entry is at the close of the **next** trading session.
- `activist_13d` is not measured here: the historical datasets used only cover Forms 3/4/5.
- Amendments (Form 4/A) are left out: they repeat transactions already reported.

## Limitations

1. **Survivorship bias.** 2,369 of 9,018 signals (26%) were discarded without being measured: 1,459 for a ticker without prices on Yahoo, 427 without a ticker, 303 without an entry price, 176 for an incompatible price and 4 for a suspect jump. Yahoo keeps no history for almost any delisted company. Delisting is either bankruptcy (a loss near 100%) or an acquisition (usually at a premium), and this backtest does not know which one happened in each case. The real result may be better or worse than reported; what can be said is that a large slice of the signals got no answer.
2. **Delisted along the way.** Signals with a measured entry but no exit price are left out only of the affected horizon: 0 at 1m, 0 at 3m, 0 at 6m.
3. **Illiquid microcaps.** 1,491 of the measured signals are stocks bought below $5.00. In them the spread and the lack of volume cost much more than the 0.2% assumed. Prefer the filtered tables when deciding.
4. **Market cap is approximate** and only exists for 79% of the measured signals (companies with more than one share class or without cover XBRL have none).
5. **Non-independent observations.** Signals close in time rise and fall together, and the 3 and 6 month horizons overlap. The monthly cohort t corrects part of this, not all of it: treat any t near 2 as weak.
6. **Benchmark.** SPY is made of large companies and was exceptionally strong in 2020–2025; part of the negative excess is a size effect. The table against IWM helps separate the two.
7. **Idealized execution.** Assumes buying and selling exactly at the close, with no price impact, no size limit and a fixed cost. Taxes and currency are not modeled.
8. **Code P is not only exchange trading.** Purchases in public offerings or private placements also carry code P, at a fixed price. The backtest cannot tell them apart from exchange purchases.
9. **Hand-entered data.** Ticker, role and price are typed by the filer. Obvious errors are filtered (price outside the 0.5x–2.0x range of the market), subtle ones get through. CEO/CFO identification relies on the role text.
10. **Yahoo adjustment errors.** When Yahoo misses a reverse split, the series shows a gain that never existed. The suspect-jump filter catches the typical case (a rise of 5x or more in one day, starting below $1.00); smaller errors get through, and unrecorded splits create false losses that cannot be told apart from real drops. That is why the median and the winsorized mean are more reliable than the plain mean.
11. **Current ticker, not the historical one.** The ticker comes from the current SEC registry when it exists (otherwise from the Form 4). Companies that changed ticker are found; those that disappeared are not.
12. **No out-of-sample parameter tuning.** The thresholds are the ones from the brief and were not optimized. If you tune them while looking at this report, set aside a period to validate them afterwards.
